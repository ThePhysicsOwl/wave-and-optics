## Pre-Class Quiz

### Q1
What is periodic motion?

A. Any motion that repeats itself after a specific time interval.  
B. Any motion that moves back and forth about an equilibrium position.  
C. Any motion that is driven by a constant force.  
D. Any motion that has a constant acceleration.  

**Answer:** A  
**Reason:** Periodic motion is defined strictly as any motion that repeats itself after a fixed time interval (period $T$).

### Q2
What is Oscillation?

A. Any motion that repeats itself along a path.  
B. The repeated variation of a physical quantity about an equilibrium or mean value.  
C. Motion under a constant acceleration.  
D. The movement of a particle along a circular path.  

**Answer:** B  
**Reason:** Oscillation is the to-and-fro or variation of a physical quantity about an equilibrium value.

### Q3
Is circular motion an example of periodic motion?

A. Yes, because it repeats itself after a fixed time interval (period).  
B. No, because it does not move back and forth about an equilibrium position.  
C. Yes, but only if the speed is constantly increasing.  
D. No, because the path is two-dimensional.  

**Answer:** A  
**Reason:** Circular motion at a constant speed repeats itself regularly and is therefore periodic, though it is not an oscillation since it doesn't move back and forth about an equilibrium position.

### Q4
What is harmonic oscillation?

A. An oscillation where the restoring force is constant.  
B. An oscillation where the restoring force is directly proportional to the displacement from equilibrium.  
C. Any oscillation that does not lose mechanical energy.  
D. An oscillation with a frequency that depends on the amplitude.  

**Answer:** B  
**Reason:** Harmonic oscillation is characterized by a restoring force proportional to displacement ($F = -Kx$).

### Q5
Which of the following is an example of harmonic oscillation?

A. A bouncing ball on a hard floor.  
B. A simple pendulum at small amplitudes.  
C. The orbit of the Earth around the Sun.  
D. A sliding block under constant kinetic friction.  

**Answer:** B  
**Reason:** For small amplitudes, the restoring force of a simple pendulum is approximately linear with displacement ($F \approx -mgx/L$), making it simple harmonic.

### Q6
What is the potential energy of a harmonic oscillator?

A. $U = \frac{1}{2}Kx$  
B. $U = Kx^2$  
C. $U = \frac{1}{2}Kx^2$  
D. $U = mgh$  

**Answer:** C  
**Reason:** The potential energy of a simple harmonic oscillator is quadratic with displacement ($U = \frac{1}{2}Kx^2$).

### Q7
What is a phasor?

A. A device that measures the phase constant of an oscillator.  
B. A rotating vector in the complex plane representing a sinusoidal quantity.  
C. A wave packet that moves through a medium.  
D. The stun ray gun used in Star Trek.  

**Answer:** B  
**Reason:** A phasor is a rotating vector (or complex number $A e^{i(\omega t + \phi)}$) whose projection or real part represents a sinusoidal wave or oscillation.

### Q8
What is time-translation invariance?

A. The property where shifting the time origin does not change the physical laws governing the system.  
B. The rule that the period of an oscillator is independent of its mass.  
C. The phenomenon where an oscillator never loses energy over time.  
D. The absolute nature of time across different frames of reference.  

**Answer:** A  
**Reason:** Time-translation invariance means that if $x(t)$ is a solution to the equation of motion, then $x(t - t_0)$ is also a solution for any time shift $t_0$, meaning the laws of physics do not change over time.

### Q9
What is linearity?

A. The property where the restoring force is constant.  
B. The property of a differential equation where any linear combination of solutions is also a solution.  
C. The rule that the path of the oscillator must be a straight line.  
D. The relation that frequency is inversely proportional to period.  

**Answer:** B  
**Reason:** In a linear system (or linear differential equation), if $x_1(t)$ and $x_2(t)$ are solutions, then $c_1 x_1(t) + c_2 x_2(t)$ is also a solution.

### Q10
What is superposition?

A. The addition of potential and kinetic energy to find the total mechanical energy.  
B. The phenomenon where the net response of a linear system is the sum of individual responses.  
C. The state of an oscillator having maximum amplitude.  
D. The transfer of wave energy between different media.  

**Answer:** B  
**Reason:** Superposition is a direct consequence of linearity, stating that when multiple inputs or waves act on a system, the net response is the sum of the individual responses.

### Q11
What is amplitude?

A. The total distance covered by the oscillator in one cycle.  
B. The maximum magnitude of displacement of the oscillator from its equilibrium position.  
C. The starting position of the oscillator at $t=0$.  
D. The number of oscillations per second.  

**Answer:** B  
**Reason:** Amplitude ($A$) is the maximum displacement from the equilibrium position.

### Q12
What is period?

A. The time taken to complete one full cycle of periodic motion.  
B. The number of cycles completed in one second.  
C. The time it takes for the oscillator to return to the origin.  
D. The duration of the entire experiment.  

**Answer:** A  
**Reason:** The period ($T$) is the duration of one complete cycle of the motion.

### Q13
What is frequency?

A. The time it takes to complete one cycle.  
B. The number of complete cycles or oscillations per unit time.  
C. The speed of the wave propagation.  
D. The phase offset at $t=0$.  

**Answer:** B  
**Reason:** Frequency ($f = 1/T$) is the number of cycles per second, measured in Hertz (Hz).

### Q14
What is phase?

A. The maximum value of the displacement.  
B. The angular argument of the sinusoidal function describing the motion at any time $t$.  
C. The initial angle of the phasor at $t=0$.  
D. The rate of change of angular frequency.  

**Answer:** B  
**Reason:** The phase ($\theta(t) = \omega t + \phi$) is the argument of the cosine/sine function and determines the exact state of the oscillator in its cycle.

### Q15
What is angular frequency?

A. The rate of change of the angle of a rotating phasor, equal to $2\pi f$.  
B. The frequency measured in cycles per minute.  
C. The angle of displacement of a pendulum.  
D. The phase angle at $t = 0$.  

**Answer:** A  
**Reason:** Angular frequency ($\omega = 2\pi f = 2\pi/T$) represents the rate of phase change in radians per second.

### Q16
What is restoring force?

A. A force that acts to restore the original mechanical energy of a system.  
B. A force that always acts in the direction opposite to the displacement, tending to return the system to equilibrium.  
C. The force exerted by gravity on a pendulum bob at the bottom of its swing.  
D. The external force that drives an oscillator at resonance.  

**Answer:** B  
**Reason:** Restoring force always pulls the system back toward its equilibrium position (i.e. opposite to the displacement).

### Q17
When is a particle/system in equilibrium?

A. When the kinetic energy equals the potential energy.  
B. When the net external force acting on the particle/system is zero.  
C. When the particle is at its maximum displacement.  
D. When the system is vibrating at its natural frequency.  

**Answer:** B  
**Reason:** Equilibrium is defined as the position where the net force (or net torque) on the system is zero.

### Q18
What is euler's formula?

A. $e^{ix} = \cos x + i \sin x$  
B. $e^{ix} = \cos x - i \sin x$  
C. $e^{x} = 1 + x + \frac{x^2}{2}$  
D. $F - E + V = 2$  

**Answer:** A  
**Reason:** Euler's formula is $e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$, relating complex exponentials to trigonometric functions.

### Q19
Is circular motion an example of harmonic oscillation?
A. Yes, because it repeats itself after a fixed time interval (period).  
B. No, because it does not move back and forth about an equilibrium position.  
C. Yes, but only if the speed is constantly increasing.  
D. No, because the path is two-dimensional.  

**Answer:** B  
**Reason:** Circular motion at a constant speed repeats itself regularly and is therefore periodic, though it is not an oscillation since it doesn't move back and forth about an equilibrium position.

### Q20
How does the presence of gravity affect the frequency of oscillation of a vertical mass-spring system compared to the same system placed horizontally?

A. It increases the frequency because gravity adds an additional restoring force.  
B. It decreases the frequency because gravity acts against the spring.  
C. It does not affect the frequency; gravity only shifts the static equilibrium position.  
D. It makes the frequency dependent on the amplitude of oscillation.  

**Answer:** C  
**Reason:** In a vertical mass-spring system, gravity only shifts the equilibrium position down by $y_0 = mg/k$. Once the displacement is measured relative to this new equilibrium, the equation of motion is identical to the horizontal system, meaning the frequency remains $\omega = \sqrt{k/m}$.
