# PHY 104 Periodic Motion Assignment Key

Instructor: Albert Ndulue

This key emphasizes reasoning targets. Accept equivalent explanations and clear diagrams.

## 1. Restoring Force

At \(x=+A\), velocity is zero at the release instant, spring force and acceleration point left. At \(x=0\), spring force and acceleration are zero, but speed is maximum. At \(x=-A\), velocity is zero at the turning point, spring force and acceleration point right.

The block does not stop permanently at equilibrium because it has kinetic energy and inertia there.

## 2. Turning Point Misconception

The statement is false. At the end of the swing, the instantaneous velocity is zero, but gravity still has a tangential component toward equilibrium. Therefore the acceleration is not zero. Velocity describes how position changes; acceleration describes how velocity changes.

## 3. Proportional Reasoning

System B has the longer period. Since \(T=2\pi\sqrt{m/k}\), quadrupling \(m\) doubles \(T\). Conceptually, the same spring force must accelerate a larger mass, so the motion responds more slowly.

## 4. Phase in Everyday Language

The students have the same frequency if they complete laps at the same rate. They are not in phase because they occupy different positions in the cycle at the same time. The phase constant \(\phi\) records that starting offset.

## 5. Energy Bar Charts

For total energy \(E=\frac12 kA^2\):

- At \(x=-A\): \(U=E\), \(K=0\).
- At \(x=0\): \(U=0\), \(K=E\).
- At \(x=+A/2\): \(U=\frac14E\), \(K=\frac34E\).

Total mechanical energy is constant in all three cases for an ideal oscillator.

## 6. Pendulum Model Validity

The \(5^\circ\) release is better described by SHM. The approximation is \(\sin\theta\approx\theta\), with \(\theta\) measured in radians. At \(70^\circ\), \(\sin\theta\) differs significantly from \(\theta\), so the restoring torque is not proportional to angular displacement.

## 7. Damping and Thermal Energy

The car body provides much of the inertia. Springs in the suspension provide the restoring effect. Shock absorbers and internal friction convert mechanical energy into thermal energy, so the oscillation amplitude decays.

## 8. Resonance in Engineering

Answers vary. Look for:

- The oscillating part or field is identified.
- Inertia and restoring effect are named.
- Resonance is classified as useful or dangerous with a reason.
- Control strategy includes changing stiffness, mass, damping, geometry, or driving frequency.

Examples: musical instrument resonance is useful; bridge/building resonance is usually dangerous; quartz resonance is useful for timing.

## 9. Graph Interpretation

For \(x(t)=A\cos(\omega t)\):

- \(t=0\): \(x=+A\), \(v=0\), \(a=-\omega^2A\).
- \(t=T/4\): \(x=0\), \(v=-A\omega\), \(a=0\).
- \(t=T/2\): \(x=-A\), \(v=0\), \(a=+\omega^2A\).

Signs depend on the chosen positive axis; here positive \(x\) is the direction of \(+A\).

## 10. Bridge to Waves

Strong answers explain that a wave is a coordinated disturbance in which one part of a medium or field oscillates and interacts with neighboring parts, passing energy and information along. Examples include a string segment pulling nearby segments, air parcels compressing neighboring air parcels in sound, and electromagnetic fields sustaining each other in light.

## Grading Notes

Prioritize conceptual consistency over algebraic length. Common high-value feedback:

- "Velocity zero does not imply acceleration zero."
- "Use the restoring-force direction to decide acceleration."
- "Phase is about where the system starts in the cycle."
- "The small-angle approximation is a modeling choice, not a universal pendulum truth."
