# PHY 104 Assignment: Periodic Motion

Instructor: Albert Ndulue  
Due: Next class  
Purpose: Test conceptual understanding of oscillations, not formula memorization.

## Instructions

Answer in complete sentences. Use equations where they clarify your reasoning, but every equation must be accompanied by an explanation. You may include sketches, graphs, or energy bar charts.

## Questions

### 1. Restoring Force

A block attached to a spring is pulled to the right and released from rest.

1. Draw the block at \(x=+A\), \(x=0\), and \(x=-A\).
2. At each position, indicate the direction of velocity, acceleration, and spring force.
3. Explain why the block does not simply stop permanently when it reaches equilibrium.

### 2. Turning Point Misconception

Critique this statement:

"At the end of a swing, the pendulum bob stops, so the acceleration must be zero."

Your answer should distinguish velocity, force, and acceleration.

### 3. Proportional Reasoning

Two mass-spring systems use the same spring. System B has four times the mass of System A.

1. Which system has the longer period?
2. By what factor?
3. Explain your answer using inertia and restoring force, not only the formula.

### 4. Phase in Everyday Language

Two students walk around a circular track at the same steady pace. Student A starts at the north point. Student B starts at the east point.

1. Are they moving with the same frequency?
2. Are they in phase?
3. Use this analogy to explain the phase constant \(\phi\) in \(x=A\cos(\omega t+\phi)\).

### 5. Energy Bar Charts

For an ideal mass-spring oscillator, draw energy bar charts for:

1. \(x=-A\)
2. \(x=0\)
3. \(x=+A/2\)

Each chart should show kinetic energy, spring potential energy, and total mechanical energy.

### 6. Pendulum Model Validity

A pendulum is released once from \(5^\circ\) and once from \(70^\circ\).

1. Which release is better described by simple harmonic motion?
2. What approximation is being used?
3. Why does the approximation fail for the larger angle?

### 7. Damping and Thermal Energy

A car goes over a speed bump and then settles after a few oscillations.

1. What plays the role of inertia?
2. What plays the role of the restoring effect?
3. Where does the mechanical energy go as the motion dies away?

### 8. Resonance in Engineering

Choose one engineered system from your environment: a bridge, building, speaker, phone, clock, vehicle suspension, washing machine, or musical instrument.

Explain:

1. What is oscillating?
2. What provides inertia?
3. What provides the restoring effect?
4. Is resonance useful or dangerous in this system?
5. How might an engineer control the oscillation?

### 9. Graph Interpretation

An oscillator has displacement \(x(t)=A\cos(\omega t)\).

1. At \(t=0\), what are the displacement, velocity, and acceleration?
2. At \(t=T/4\), what are the displacement, velocity, and acceleration?
3. At \(t=T/2\), what are the displacement, velocity, and acceleration?

You may answer using signs and relative magnitudes rather than numbers.

### 10. Bridge to Waves

In one paragraph, explain this statement:

"A wave is what happens when many oscillators are coupled."

Use an example such as a string, water surface, sound in air, or light in an optical cavity.
